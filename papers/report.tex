\begin{abstract}
This report assesses the monetary and non-monetary value of Minnesota family forests, with particular attention to the ecosystem services they provide. First, it establishes a framework for evaluating these services in both ecological and economic terms. Then, for a selection of high priority ecosystem services I estimate the economic value attributable to Minnesota forests. I demonstrate heterogeneity in value across forest conditions, ownership types, and across the time horizion 2003 - 2023. This report therefore provides both a conceptual framework and empirical analysis for understanding the value of Minnesota family forests.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Family Forests; Ecosystem Services; Forest Valuation; Minnesota

\subsubsection{From FIA observations to spatial forest layers}

The spatial foundation of the assessment combines the U.S.\ Forest Service Forest Inventory and Analysis (FIA) database for Minnesota with European Space Agency (ESA) land-cover masks. This provides information on historical research plots, forest conditions, tree characteristics, and population records. Annual evaluation coverage for 2000--2025 is documented using \texttt{POP\_EVAL.END\_INVYR}; spatial composites instead use the actual measurement year, \texttt{MEASYEAR}. These are distinct temporal definitions. The layers represent forests across Minnesota, not exclusively family-owned forests. Attribution to family forests is derived from propritoary ownership information accessed through MFRC. Basal area provides a structural density proxy for service-specific models.

Figure~\ref{fig:forest-layer-construction} illustrates the transformation of FIA plots into a smoothed interpolation of forest cover. Panel A summarizes distinct measurement years during 2000--2025 for each physical plot.
% , identified by the combination of \texttt{STATECD}, \texttt{UNITCD}, \texttt{COUNTYCD}, and \texttt{PLOT}, rather than by the visit-specific identifier \texttt{CN}. 
Each site is displayed once at its latest public location, and all plot statuses are included in this sampling overview. Seventy records lacking a measurement year are excluded from the temporal summary. Among 18,286 physical sites with measurements, 80.62\%, were observed in five distinct years and 17.63\% in six (Table~\ref{tab:fia-frequency}). Measurement frequency describes the available records, not an assumed fixed remeasurement interval.

I condense the this temporal variation into 5 year time steps $t\in\{2005,2010,2015,2020,2025\}$, observations are restricted to the trailing five measurement years $[t-4,t]$. Only the newest visit per physical plot is retained, ordered by measurement year, month, day, inventory year, and visit identifier; unknown month or day values precede known values. Selection precedes forest-status filtering, so an earlier forest observation is not substituted for a newer nonforest or nonsampled visit. Sampled plots (\texttt{PLOT\_STATUS\_CD}=1) with forest conditions (\texttt{COND\_STATUS\_CD}=1) contribute basal area. For plot $p$, the forest-area-normalized response is
\begin{equation}
B_{p,t} =
\frac{\sum_{c\in F_p}\mathrm{BALIVE}_{pc,t}\,w_{pc,t}}
     {\sum_{c\in F_p}w_{pc,t}},
\qquad
w_{pc,t}=\mathrm{CONDPROP\_UNADJ}_{pc,t},
\label{eq:plot-basal-area}
\end{equation}
where $F_p$ denotes forest conditions. The resulting quantity is square feet of live-tree basal area per forest acre, not per total plot acre. Plots with invalid basal area or forest-condition proportions, invalid coordinates, or locations outside Minnesota are excluded. Public coordinates are transformed from geographic coordinates to NAD83 / UTM zone 15N (EPSG:26915); coincident locations, if present, are averaged to avoid duplicate spatial weighting and singular kriging systems. Table~\ref{tab:forest-layer-samples} records the sampling windows and retained forest sites.

Each composite is interpolated separately using local ordinary kriging. An exponential semivariogram,
\begin{equation}
\gamma_t(h)=\tau_t^2+\sigma_t^2\{1-\exp(-h/a_t)\},\qquad h>0,
\label{eq:forest-variogram}
\end{equation}
is fitted to a reproducible random sample of up to 1,000 eligible sites.
% (seed 42). 
Here $\tau_t^2$ is the nugget, $\sigma_t^2$ the partial sill, and $a_t$ the exponential scale. Pairwise semivariances are grouped into 20 equal-width distance bins; bins with fewer than 30 pairs are omitted, and bounded nonlinear least squares weights the residuals by the square root of each bin's share of pairs. Predictions use the nearest 32 of all eligible sites, not just the variogram-fitting subset. Ordinary-kriging weights sum to one; the nugget is included in observation covariance but not added to the covariance of the predicted latent surface. Negative predictions are clipped to zero, and the second raster band stores latent kriging variance.

Predictions are evaluated on a common 300-metre EPSG:26915 grid clipped to the Minnesota county boundary. ESA classes 50, 60, 61, 62, 70, 71, 72, 80, 81, 82, 90, and 100 define forest; mosaics and flooded-forest classes are excluded. Categorical land cover is aligned by nearest-neighbor resampling. 
% All eligible forest pixels are predicted, including locations beyond the sampled spatial support; other pixels are NoData rather than zero. 
The 2025 composite explicitly uses the 2022 ESA mask because a 2025 mask is not avaliable. Panel B displays the masked 2025 basal-area surface; Panel C displays its exact ESA forest footprint, recovered from valid prediction cells so that an additional reprojection cannot change the mask. Gray areas include both land outside the forest mask and unavailable land cover.

Panel D compares basal-area density using
\begin{equation}
\Delta B_i(\%) =
100\,\frac{\widehat B_{i,2025}-\widehat B_{i,2005}}
               {\widehat B_{i,2005}},
\label{eq:forest-density-change}
\end{equation}
only where cell $i$ is forested in both saved masks and $\widehat B_{i,2005}>0$. Nonoverlapping forest pixels and zero baselines are excluded rather than assigned zero change. This comparison measures structural density change between five-year composites.
% , not forest-cover change, annual sequestration, or growth in ecosystem-service value. 
Small baselines can amplify percentage changes.

The resulting layers are exploratory spatial inputs, not FIA design-based population estimates: no population expansion weights are used in the interpolation. Public FIA coordinates are fuzzed or swapped, and 300-metre cell size does not establish 300-metre positional accuracy. The fitted variograms have not been spatially cross-validated; latent kriging variance omits land-cover, temporal, and coordinate uncertainty. Consequently, apparent local change should be interpreted cautiously, and service-specific quantity models, ownership attribution, and validation are required before these layers support estimates of family-forest ecosystem-service values.

\subsubsection{Forest Ownership}

Forest ownership provides a basis for examining heterogeneity in forest conditions and ecosystem services. Figure~\ref{fig:forest-ownership} maps the \href{https://doi.org/10.2737/RDS-2025-0045}{Harris, Caputo, and Butler (2025)} forest ownership dataset for Minnesota. Ownership is classified circa 2022 using parcel and other ownership data, while the forest-cover classification is based on 2016 canopy data. The six mapped ownership classes are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Family:} families, individuals, trusts, estates, family partnerships, and other unincorporated groups of individuals.
\item \textbf{Corporate/other private:} corporations and other private organizations, including conservation and natural-resource organizations, partnerships, and associations. This class includes, but does not identify separately, forest industry.
\item \textbf{Tribal:} forest classified using Tribal land boundaries. This boundary-based classification does not establish parcel-level ownership.
\item \textbf{Federal, state, and local:} forests classified under the respective levels of public ownership.
\end{itemize}
Unknown forest ownership is retained as a separate category, rather than assigned to any group. Nonforest and water are also displayed for context.

\subsubsection{Ecosystem 1: Timber Provision}


\paragraph{Data and valuation approach.}
The first-pass spatial estimate uses the 2025 Minnesota forest raster (300-m cells, with the 2022 ESA forest mask) as its footprint. FIA's \texttt{rFIA::growMort} supplies species-level, plot-level annual growth, recruitment, natural mortality, and removals for growing-stock trees on forest land. The analysis retains the latest eligible visit per physical plot in the 2021--2025 measurement window; 5,655 plot visits at unique coordinates remain. For each plot and species, the maximum sustainable harvest ceiling is defined as nonnegative survivor growth plus recruitment into the measured class minus natural mortality:
\[
q_{p,s}^{\mathrm{sust}}=
\max\left(G_{p,s}+R_{p,s}-M_{p,s},0\right),
\]
where $G$, $R$, and $M$ are the FIA annualized growth, recruitment, and mortality rates, respectively. Observed FIA removals are retained as a sustainability diagnostic and are not subtracted from the ceiling. Plot-level species values are aggregated to annual product quantities and value per hectare, then local ordinary kriging with 32 nearest sites interpolates those rates onto forest pixels.

The FIA growing-stock net cubic-foot ceiling is converted to cord products using 80 solid cubic feet per cord, then the sawtimber equivalent is removed using the DNR report's conversion of two cords per Mbf. FIA sawtimber board-foot rates are divided by 1,000 to obtain Mbf. Negative residual cord quantities are set to zero. This partitions the merchantable quantity into a cord-product proxy and sawtimber; it is not a detailed log-grade classification. The species groups are crosswalked to Minnesota DNR's 2021 public-agency average stumpage prices:
\[
Q_i^{\mathrm{cord}}=A_iq_i^{\mathrm{cord,sust}},
\qquad
Q_i^{\mathrm{saw}}=A_iq_i^{\mathrm{saw,sust}},
\qquad
V_i^{\mathrm{stumpage}}=
Q_i^{\mathrm{cord}}P_{s,\mathrm{cord}}+
Q_i^{\mathrm{saw}}P_{s,\mathrm{saw}},
\]
where $A_i$ is forest-pixel area and prices are in 2021 nominal dollars per cord or per Mbf, respectively. Total mapped value is
\[
\widehat V_{\mathrm{timber}}=\sum_i V_i^{\mathrm{stumpage}}.
\]
The price source is the Minnesota DNR \href{https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/forestry/timbersales/stumpage.html}{Public Stumpage Price Review}; the latest listed report is 2021. Prices are public-agency averages by species group and product. The DNR reports that the series covers approximately 80\% of commercial timber sales issued by Minnesota public agencies.

\subsubsection{Ecosystem 2: Carbon Sequestration}


\paragraph{Data.}
Use the Minnesota FIA forest raster as the spatial base. Estimate change in carbon stocks per hectare from remeasured FIA plots or FIA annual estimates, accounting for growth, mortality, and removals over the measurement interval. Record the included carbon pools, inventory years, and cell area $A_i$; use a consistent pool definition across inventories. Obtain a social cost of carbon or carbon price in dollars per tCO2e.

\paragraph{Valuation Approach.}
For pixel $i$, calculate annual net sequestration from repeated FIA carbon inventories, multiplying by pixel area and converting tC to tCO$_2$e:
\[
Q_i=A_i\left(\frac{C_{i,t_2}-C_{i,t_1}}{t_2-t_1}\right)\left(\frac{44}{12}\right),
\]
where the same carbon pools are included in both inventories. $\left(\frac{44}{12}\right)$ is molecular-weight conversion from carbon ($C$) to carbon dioxide ($CO_2$). 
Carbon has an atomic mass of about 12. 
$CO_2$ has a molecular mass of about $44:12$ for carbon $+ 16 \times 2$ for oxygen.
So 1 tonne of carbon $= 44/12 \approx 3.667$ tonnes of $CO_2$.
Select a social cost of carbon or carbon-market price, stated in a consistent dollar year:
\[
P_i=P_{\mathrm{CO_2}}\quad [\$/\mathrm{tCO_2e}].
\]

Currently, set nature's contribution to be fully attributable to the eligible family forest:

\[
\lambda_i= 1.
\]

Value the annual flow as
\[
V_{\mathrm{sequestration}}=\sum_i Q_iP_i\lambda_i,
\]
where $Q_i$ is annual net tCO$_2$e and $P_i$ is dollars per tCO$_2$e. The result is an annual flow value; do not add the full carbon-stock value to it without defining a non-overlapping valuation period.

\paragraph{Estimated Value}

The first-pass implementation estimates signed annualized changes in live aboveground tree carbon from linked FIA plot visits, rather than differencing the independently kriged storage maps. For a matched physical plot $p$, the observed rate is
\begin{equation}
r_p=\frac{C^{\mathrm{AG}}_{p,\mathrm{current}}-
                C^{\mathrm{AG}}_{p,\mathrm{previous}}}
               {\mathrm{REMPER}_p}
\quad [\mathrm{tC/ha/year}],
\label{eq:matched-carbon-rate}
\end{equation}
where \texttt{PLOT.REMPER} is the FIA remeasurement interval in years. Each visit's live aboveground stock is calculated from live forest-tree \texttt{CARBON\_AG} multiplied by \texttt{TPA\_UNADJ}, normalized by the forest-condition proportion and converted to tonnes C/ha. Negative rates are retained as live-pool losses. Local ordinary kriging produces $\widehat r_{i,t}$ on the existing forest grid, and the valued rate is
\begin{equation}
v^{\mathrm{annual}}_{i,t}
=\widehat r_{i,t}\left(\frac{44}{12}\right)P_t
\quad [\mathrm{2007\ USD/ha/year}].
\label{eq:implemented-carbon-sequestration}
\end{equation}
Raster values are per hectare per year; statewide annualized totals multiply each eligible 300-metre cell by 9 hectares.

\subsubsection{Ecosystem 3: Carbon Storage}


\paragraph{Data.}
The spatial base is the Minnesota FIA forest raster, with pixel area $A_i$. Required attributes are FIA carbon stocks per hectare by pool (live trees, deadwood, litter, and soil where available) and the pool coverage and inventory date. Convert carbon to CO2-equivalent using $44/12$.

\paragraph{Valuation Approach.}
For pixel $i$, calculate the total carbon stock in the included pools, multiplied by pixel area and converted from tC to tCO$_2$e:
\[
Q_i=A_i C_i\left(\frac{44}{12}\right),
\]
where $C_i$ is the combined pool stock in tC/ha. Select a social cost of carbon and state its dollar year:
\[
P_i=P_{\mathrm{SCC}}\quad [\$/\mathrm{tCO_2e}].
\]
Currently, set nature's contribution to be fully attributable to the eligible family forest:

\[
\lambda_i= 1.
\]
Value the stock as
\[
V_{\mathrm{storage}}=\sum_i Q_iP_i\lambda_i,
\]
where $Q_i$ is tCO$_2$e and $P_i$ is dollars per tCO$_2$e. This is a one-time stock value, not an annual flow; keep it separate from sequestration value to avoid double counting.

\paragraph{Estimated Value}

The first-pass implementation values live aboveground tree carbon in the five kriged forest composites, rather than the combined carbon pools proposed above. The per-hectare stock-value proxy for cell $i$ and composite year $t$ is
\begin{equation}
v_{i,t}=\widehat C^{\mathrm{AG}}_{i,t}\left(\frac{44}{12}\right)P_t
\quad [\mathrm{2007\ USD/ha}],
\label{eq:implemented-carbon-storage}
\end{equation}
where $\widehat C^{\mathrm{AG}}_{i,t}$ is live aboveground carbon in metric tonnes C per forest hectare. Pixel area is not multiplied into this value-density raster. Statewide totals subsequently multiply each valid cell by its projected area of 9 hectares. The mean value per hectare is the total stock-value proxy divided by that year's mapped forest area, excluding NoData cells rather than treating them as zero.

\subsubsection{Ecosystem 4: Habitat Support for Biodiversity}


\paragraph{Data.}
Use the Minnesota FIA forest raster as the spatial base. Required forest attributes include FIA species composition, tree size and basal area, and available structural measures such as snags and down wood; add species range or occurrence data for the selected taxonomic groups. Derive a species-specific habitat suitability score $H_i$ for each pixel using a stated habitat model, then multiply by cell area $A_i$. Obtain a species-specific willingness-to-pay or restoration-cost estimate $u_i$ per quality-adjusted hectare and estimate the forest-attributable share $\lambda_i$.

\paragraph{Valuation Approach.}
Calculate quality-adjusted habitat area for species $s$ and pixel $i$:
\[
Q_{is}=A_i H_{is},
\]
where $H_{is}$ is the habitat-suitability score from FIA composition/structure and species range or occurrence data. Select a species-specific willingness-to-pay or restoration-cost estimate per quality-adjusted hectare:
\[
P_s=u_s\quad [\$/\mathrm{quality\mbox{-}adjusted\ ha}].
\]
Estimate the fraction of suitable habitat attributable to forest by comparing the modeled forest condition with its nonforest counterfactual:
\[
\lambda_{is}=\frac{\max(0,H_{is}^{\mathrm{forest}}-H_{is}^{\mathrm{counterfactual}})}{H_{is}^{\mathrm{forest}}},\quad H_{is}^{\mathrm{forest}}>0.
\]
Then value habitat as
\[
V_{\mathrm{habitat}}=\sum_{i,s} Q_{is}P_s\lambda_{is}.
\]
Keep the valuation basis consistent across species and avoid summing overlapping values for the same habitat. If using restoration costs, report them as a cost-based proxy rather than as willingness to pay.

\paragraph{Estimated Value}

The first-pass implementation values statewide \emph{nongame habitat quality} using breeding-bird community diversity as an indicator, rather than species-specific habitat-suitability models. The quantity proxy comes from the USGS North American Breeding Bird Survey (BBS) 2026 data release (1966--2025). Quality-criteria route-years (\texttt{RunType} $=1$, standard protocol \texttt{RPID} $=101$, roadside routes) within 100 km of Minnesota are restricted to diurnal landbird orders, excluding waterbirds, shorebirds, raptors, nocturnal species, and unidentified or hybrid codes. For each route-year $r$, counts are summed across the route's 50 stops and converted to effective species (the Hill number of order one),
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{effN}_r=\exp\!\left(-\sum_s p_{rs}\ln p_{rs}\right),
\label{eq:effective-species}
\end{equation}
where $p_{rs}$ is species $s$'s share of route-year $r$'s individuals. A spatiotemporal generalized additive model is fitted to 5,928 route-years from 184 routes,
\begin{equation}
\log \mathrm{effN}_r=\beta_0+\beta_1 F_r+f(x_r,y_r,\mathrm{year}_r)+b_{o(r)}+\varepsilon_r,
\label{eq:diversity-gam}
\end{equation}
with a tensor-product smooth $f$ over space and time, a first-year-observer indicator $F_r$, and observer random effects $b_o$ absorbing systematic detection differences (82.2\% deviance explained). Population-level predictions, excluding observer terms, are evaluated on the 300-metre forest grid. Habitat-quality change for pixel $i$ in target year $t$ is the percentage change of predicted effective species relative to that pixel's 1966--1970 geometric mean prediction,
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{i,t}=100\left(\frac{\widehat{\mathrm{effN}}_{i,t}}{\overline{\mathrm{effN}}_{i,1966\mbox{\scriptsize --}1970}}-1\right)\quad [\%].
\label{eq:habitat-quality-change}
\end{equation}
The \href{https://mnforcast.org/ecosystem-valuation}{ForCAST choice experiment} estimates willingness to pay (WTP) of \$1.15 per household per month for a 10\% increase in nongame species habitat quality (Table~1). To construct an illustrative level-based GEP proxy, define $B$ as the area-weighted mean of the 1966--1970 geometric mean predictions on 2005 forest pixels whose centers fall in the 11 survey counties. The 2005 mask is the earliest available, not historical forest cover. This chosen baseline is not reported by the survey. A 10\% increase corresponds to $0.1B$ effective species, yielding an annual price of $p=1.15\times 12/(0.1B)$ per household per effective species. With $HH_t$ Minnesota households and total mapped forest area $A^F_t$ in hectares, the value-density raster is
\begin{equation}
v_{i,t}=\frac{HH_t\times p\times \widehat{\mathrm{effN}}_{i,t}}{A^F_t}
\quad [\mathrm{USD/ha/year}],
\label{eq:implemented-biodiversity}
\end{equation}
so that summing $v_{i,t}$ over forest pixels (9 hectares each) reproduces the statewide level proxy. This extrapolates the local +10\% WTP price linearly all the way to zero diversity; it is not measured total WTP, a market price, or a demonstrated forest-attributable value. The source explicitly finds nonlinear, loss-averse preferences; the extrapolation and the transfer from nongame habitat quality to bird diversity and from the survey counties to all Minnesota households are substantial assumptions. Household counts are year-specific Census estimates (ACS 1-year table B11001 for 2005, 2010, 2015, and 2025; the 2020 Decennial Census for 2020).

Table~\ref{tab:biodiversity-results} summarizes the five composites. The survey-area anchor is approximately 29.92 effective species, giving an illustrative price of \$4.61 per household per effective species per year. For 2025, predicted forest diversity averages 27.10 effective species, yielding a level proxy of approximately \textbf{\$298.94 million/year}, or \$49.86/ha/year. The increase in the proxy from 2005 reflects household growth; at fixed 2005 households the 2025 proxy would be approximately \$252.48 million/year.

\subsubsection{Ecosystem 9: Air Quality Purification (Regulation)}

\paragraph{Data.}
Use the Minnesota FIA forest raster as the spatial base, with FIA species and stand attributes that inform leaf area and pollutant removal. Couple it with canopy or i-Tree pollutant-removal estimates for PM$_{2.5}$, ozone, NO$_2$, and SO$_2$, ambient pollution and meteorology, exposed-population data, and pollutant-specific marginal health-damage estimates (for example, from EPA BenMAP). Estimate $\lambda_{ip}$, the share of avoided damages attributable to forest removal, accounting for where and when removal changes human exposure.

\paragraph{Valuation Approach.}
Calculate annual pollutant removal for pixel $i$ and pollutant $p$ from canopy/i-Tree rates:
\[
Q_{ip}=A_i r_{ip},
\]
where $r_{ip}$ is tons removed per hectare per year; use atmospheric transport/exposure modeling to refine the quantity where available. Estimate marginal health damages avoided per ton using pollutant-specific concentration-response functions, exposed-population data, and health-damage values (for example, BenMAP):
\[
P_{ip}=MD_{ip}\quad [\$/\mathrm{ton\ pollutant}].
\]
Estimate the share of modeled damage reduction attributable to eligible family-forest canopy using the ownership overlay and a no-canopy counterfactual:
\[
\lambda_{ip}=\frac{\Delta D_{ip}^{\mathrm{eligible\ forest}}}{\Delta D_{ip}^{\mathrm{all\ canopy}}},
\]
where $\Delta D$ is avoided health damage and the denominator is positive. If $Q_{ip}$ already measures only the eligible-forest counterfactual difference, set $\lambda_{ip}=1$ to avoid applying attribution twice. Then value annual air-quality benefits as
\[
V_{\mathrm{air}}=\sum_{i,p} Q_{ip}P_{ip}\lambda_{ip},
\]
using consistent units and avoiding counting the same exposure benefit under multiple services.

\subsubsection{Ecosystem 10: Water Quality Regulation from Riparian Buffers}

\paragraph{Data.}
Use the Minnesota FIA forest raster to identify forest cover, species composition, and stand attributes, and combine it with a complete land-use/land-cover raster so InVEST Nutrient Delivery Ratio (NDR) can represent both forest and nonforest sources. Required inputs include a stream network and watershed boundaries, a hydrologically conditioned DEM, a runoff proxy, and an NDR biophysical table with nitrogen and phosphorus loads and retention efficiencies by land-cover class. Delineate riparian buffers using a stated stream-distance/width rule and identify family-forest ownership within them. Run NDR for the observed land cover and a counterfactual in which the targeted riparian forest buffers are replaced by the specified nonforest cover; keep all other inputs and model parameters fixed.

\paragraph{Valuation Approach.}
For nutrient $n$ and watershed $w$, calculate the annual nutrient load reduction attributable to riparian buffers from the two NDR scenarios:
\[
Q_{nw}=\max\left(0,E_{nw}^{\mathrm{no\ buffer}}-E_{nw}^{\mathrm{with\ buffer}}\right),
\]
where $E_{nw}$ is NDR-modeled nutrient export in kg/year. Select a pollutant-specific marginal avoided treatment/damage cost or nutrient-credit price:
\[
P_{nw}=p_{nw}^{\mathrm{avoided}}\quad [\$/\mathrm{kg\ nutrient}],
\]
using local treatment costs, water-quality damage estimates, or applicable nutrient-credit markets; state the price year and do not add overlapping cost measures. Attribute the modeled buffer benefit to family forests using an NDR scenario that removes only eligible family-forest buffers:
\[
\lambda_{nw}=
\frac{\Delta E_{nw}^{\mathrm{eligible\ family\ forest}}}
{\Delta E_{nw}^{\mathrm{all\ riparian\ buffers}}},
\quad
0\leq\lambda_{nw}\leq1,
\]
where each $\Delta E$ is the reduction in exported nutrient relative to its matching no-buffer scenario. If $Q_{nw}$ is already calculated using only eligible family-forest buffers, set $\lambda_{nw}=1$ to avoid applying attribution twice. Value the annual water-quality regulation service as
\[
V_{\mathrm{water}}=\sum_{n,w}Q_{nw}P_{nw}\lambda_{nw}.
\]
Use non-nested watershed outlets or otherwise prevent double counting loads, and report nitrogen and phosphorus separately where their prices or damage pathways differ.

\subsubsection{FEMA Implied Values}

\paragraph{Data.}
As a benchmark against the service-by-service estimates above, this section applies per-acre ecosystem-service values from FEMA's 2022 benefit--cost analysis update (\href{https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_ecosystem-service-value-updates_2022.pdf}{FEMA 2022}, Table~5, page~20) to the 300-metre FIA/ESA forest layers for 2005--2025. FEMA's reported \emph{Forest} and \emph{Riparian} components sum to \$12,590 and \$37,200 per acre per year, respectively, in 2021 USD. These sums differ by \$1 from FEMA's published totals of \$12,589 and \$37,199 because the components are rounded.

\paragraph{Valuation Approach.}
Riparian forest is approximated using a 50-metre buffer around selected Minnesota DNR hydrography: natural streams (perennial, intermittent, and unknown), river centerlines, lakes, ponds, reservoirs, river polygons, wetlands, inundation areas, and intermittent-water polygons. Ditches and artificial lake and wetland connectors are excluded. Water polygon interiors are excluded from the buffered area, and overlapping buffers are counted only once. The buffers are sampled on a 30-metre grid to estimate their fraction of each 300-metre forest cell. Since sub-cell forest cover is unavailable in the forest layer, forest is assumed uniformly distributed within each cell. The same DNR hydrography snapshot is used for every target year; this does not reconstruct historical water features.

The Riparian bundle \emph{replaces} the Forest bundle within the buffer. With $A_t^R$ and $A_t^N$ the estimated riparian and remaining forest acres,
\[
V_t^{\mathrm{FEMA}} = A_t^N \times \$12{,}590 + A_t^R \times \$37{,}200
\quad [\$/\mathrm{year}].
\]
The value raster (\path{data/GEP/gep-fema-implied-riparian-<year>.tif}) records the area-weighted average of the two bundles in each forest cell. Unreported FEMA service values are omitted from the respective bundle, not evidence that the services are absent; for example, no Riparian existence value is reported. The estimates are national benefit transfers without Minnesota-specific calibration, a marginal-change interpretation, or ownership attribution. They overlap services valued independently above, so the FEMA totals are a standalone benchmark and must \emph{not} be added to the other GEP estimates.

\paragraph{Estimated Value}
Table~\ref{tab:fema-implied} applies both FEMA columns to Minnesota's 2025 forest extent of 14.81 million acres (666,151 pixels on the ESA 2022 mask). An estimated 1.24 million acres (8.4\%) are riparian under the buffer and uniform-within-cell assumptions, leaving 13.57 million nonriparian acres. The combined implied value is \textbf{\$217.1 billion/year} (2021 USD): \$46.2 billion from the riparian bundle and \$170.9 billion from the remaining forest bundle. This is \$30.6 billion higher than applying the Forest bundle to all mapped forest acres, reflecting the difference between the two FEMA bundles, not an independently measured change in ecosystem services.

\subsubsection{ESVD Implied Values}

\paragraph{Data.}
As a second, highly exploratory benchmark, the \href{https://www.esvd.net/}{Ecosystem Services Valuation Database} (ESVD), version SEP2026V1.0, provides standardized observations in 2020 international dollars per hectare per year (Int\$2020/ha/year). International dollars are purchasing-power-parity-adjusted units, not nominal US dollars; these results are not directly comparable to FEMA's 2021 USD values. The database citation is Brander, de Groot, Guisado Go\~ni, van 't Hoff, Sch\"agner, Horsten, Ballell Carl, Solomonides, McVittie, Eppink, Sposato, Do, Ghermandi, and Sinclair (2026), \emph{Ecosystem Services Valuation Database}, Foundation for Sustainable Development and Brander Environmental Economics.

The forest export is restricted to observations with one service and only forest biomes, including observations covering multiple forest biomes. We retain records with a standardized value and \texttt{Value\_Change} marked \texttt{Total}, exclude records with \texttt{Incl\_Excl} equal to 0, and remove identical duplicate records by value identifier. Bundled services and mixed forest--nonforest biome records are excluded rather than attributing their full values to each component. For genetic resources, biological control, maintenance of life cycles, inspiration, and spiritual experience, no qualifying forest values are present; selected single-service, single-biome observations from the all-ecoregions export supplement these categories. The selection yields 619 forest and 120 all-biome observations across 21 services. Aesthetic information and ornamental resources lack qualifying forest values and are not supplemented; their omission is not evidence that these services are absent. The selected records are listed in \path{outputs/tables/evsd-implied-selected-records.csv}.

\paragraph{Valuation Approach.}
For each service $s$, the unweighted arithmetic mean $\overline{p}_s$ is calculated across selected observations, not across equally weighted studies. The illustrative rate and statewide total are
\[
p^{\mathrm{ESVD}}=\sum_s \overline{p}_s,\qquad
V_t^{\mathrm{ESVD}}=A_t^F p^{\mathrm{ESVD}}
\quad [\mathrm{Int\$2020/year}],
\]
where $A_t^F$ is the mapped forest area in hectares. The same rate is applied uniformly to every forest pixel and every year. There is no separate riparian rate, adjustment for forest condition, Minnesota-specific calibration, or family-forest attribution. The database's \href{https://www.esvd.info/ourdatabase}{guidance} cautions against applying general summary statistics as value transfers. Moreover, service categories and valuation methods may overlap even after excluding explicitly bundled records. The sum is therefore a sensitivity-prone illustrative calculation, not a validated total GEP estimate; it must not be added to FEMA or other service-specific estimates.

\paragraph{Estimated Value.}
The selected service means sum to \textbf{Int\$94,812/ha/year}, yielding approximately \textbf{Int\$568.4 billion/year} on the 2025 forest mask (5.995 million hectares). This number is particularly sensitive to extreme observations: the mean existence/bequest value of Int\$52,109 per hectare per year comes from only four observations, one of which is Int\$208,204/ha/year. The all-biome inspiration mean is Int\$14,509/ha/year, compared with a median of only Int\$0.093/ha/year. The arithmetic mean weights multiple observations from the same study separately, and the fallback values may not represent forest services in Minnesota.
